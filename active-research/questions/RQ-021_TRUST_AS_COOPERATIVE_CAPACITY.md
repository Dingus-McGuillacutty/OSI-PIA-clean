# RQ-021 — Trust as Cooperative Capacity and Relational State

**Domain:** OSI
**Status:** Active research
**Classification:** Exploratory; not a canonical definition

## Research question

Can organizational trust be modeled coherently as accumulated cooperative
capacity, a distributed contextual field, discrete relational transfer events,
and evolving relationship states—and do these representations improve
explanation of capability flow and organizational state transitions?

## Motivation

Trust may be more than an individual attitude or a single organizational score.
This question tests whether several bounded representations can coexist without
collapsing distinct observations into an unsupported aggregate.

## Initial subquestions

- What evidence can support accumulated cooperative capacity?
- Which contextual conditions should be represented as a field rather than a
  person-level property?
- Can trust-relevant transfer events be observed without treating every
  interaction as proof of trust?
- Which relationship states and transitions are sufficiently observable for
  governed analysis?
- Do these representations explain capability flow better than a single trust
  measure?

## Boundary and non-claims

This question does not authorize a trust score, psychological diagnosis, or
inference about an individual’s character. Any future model must preserve
provenance, uncertainty, domain boundaries, and participant or organizational
authority.

## Evidence needed for progression

Progress requires explicit operational definitions, synthetic and empirical
comparison cases, congruence review across OSI and PIA, repeatable graph or
analytic tests, and governance review before any canonical promotion.
