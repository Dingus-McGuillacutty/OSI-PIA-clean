# Machine Participation and Epistemic Stewardship

## Status and boundary

This note preserves useful language and observations emerging from the
project's external machine-participation tests. It is exploratory research
material, not a canonical principle, a governance rule, or an authorization
for machine action. The phrases below should remain available for later
question formation, comparison, and possible refinement.

## Phrases to preserve

> **Computational diversity requires human epistemic stewardship.**

This phrase names the responsibility created when multiple computational
systems produce different readings, omissions, or confidence patterns. Model
variation can be informative, but it does not remove the need for people to
compare sources, preserve disagreement, assess evidence, and remain
accountable for conclusions.

> **Conceptual autonomy is not isolation from machine influence. It is the
> developed capacity to use, hold, challenge, combine, and reject
> machine-generated information without surrendering responsibility for what
> one comes to believe.**

This phrase distinguishes human agency from machine-free work. A person may
use computational assistance while retaining responsibility for interpretation,
belief, judgment, and action. Autonomy therefore concerns the capacity to
evaluate and direct influence, not the absence of influence.

## Research implications

These phrases suggest a research strand connecting external model testing,
epistemic boundaries, human-centered computational architecture, and
orientation before optimization. Possible questions include:

- How should independent model outputs be compared without treating any one
  model as the definitive interpreter?
- What practices help people preserve disagreement and uncertainty across
  machine-generated interpretations?
- Which repository signals help a person recognize when computational
  assistance has become an unsupported conclusion?
- How can systems strengthen conceptual autonomy rather than quietly displacing
  human judgment?

The questions remain open. No claim of model superiority, human immunity from
machine influence, or general safety follows from these notes alone.
