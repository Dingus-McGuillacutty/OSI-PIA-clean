# PIA and OSI Literature Positioning Notes

## Purpose

These notes record an initial positioning pass after adding full research
reports and scholarly leads to the [Project Bibliography](../../docs/publications/PROJECT_BIBLIOGRAPHY.md).
They preserve the distinction between what existing research already supports,
what OSI-PIA may be recombining, and what remains an untested proposition.

This is a working research note, not a novelty claim or a promotion decision.
The [Literature-Positioning Matrix](../methodology/LITERATURE_POSITIONING_MATRIX.md)
is the structured comparison instrument; this note records the current
interpretive direction.

The related [PIA ↔ Open Skills / LER Crosswalk](../methodology/PIA_LER_OPEN_SKILLS_CROSSWALK.md)
keeps the interoperability question distinct from the literature-positioning
question: PIA may be richer internally while exporting only bounded,
standards-compatible claims.

## Working positioning

Existing research is already strong on skills, credentials, mobility, employer
opportunity architecture, selection outcomes, and firm-level differences in
developmental conditions. OSI-PIA should build on that work rather than claim
to have discovered those relationships.

The narrower open question is whether heterogeneous, longitudinal evidence can
make capability legible before it has been named by a credential, skill label,
title, or prior role—and whether that evidence can be translated into trusted,
developmental opportunity without losing provenance or participant control.

## PIA direction

PIA's candidate contribution is **capability inference before conventional
signaling**:

> Given a longitudinal body of heterogeneous evidence, what higher-order
> capabilities and developmental trajectories are reasonably supported, even
> where the person's institutional history has never explicitly named or
> rewarded them?

The proposed evidence chain remains bounded:

```text
career history + artifacts + projects + informal work + learning trajectory
  + contextual evidence
→ source-grounded capability model
→ plausible adjacent or higher-complexity work
→ testable developmental bridge
```

This is a research proposition. It does not establish that PIA can predict
performance, guarantee mobility, or replace selection, credential, or human
review processes.

Relevant literature branches include:

```text
human capital and signaling
→ skill utilization
→ overqualification
→ person–environment fit
→ capability approach
→ high-potential identification
→ learning agility
→ work-sample and evidence-based assessment
→ career mobility
```

## OSI direction

Research already shows that firms, roles, managers, internal labor markets, and
work organization influence mobility and development. OSI therefore should not
ask whether organizations affect development. A stronger question is:

> Which observable organizational states and mechanisms predict whether existing
> human capability will be recognized, trusted, deployed, developed, and
> translated into increasing responsibility?

This motivates **Capability Absorption Capacity** as a research construct, not
as an established metric. Candidate mechanisms include:

```text
recognition · trust · autonomy · information access · sponsorship
cross-boundary permeability · development opportunity · manager incentives
internal mobility structure
→ capability absorption and utilization
→ observable access, promotion, pay, retention, or other bounded outcomes
```

Relevant literature branches include:

```text
internal labor markets
→ talent management
→ internal mobility
→ work design
→ leadership and development
→ trust
→ psychological safety
→ knowledge and information flow
→ organizational learning
→ person–environment fit
→ human-capital utilization
```

OSI does not claim that these mechanisms have been uniquely identified by the
project. Its research opportunity is to represent more of the upstream
conditions, relationships, and state transitions behind observed outcomes while
preserving non-causal boundaries.

## Established, recombined, and open

| Category | Current working assessment |
|---|---|
| Already established | Skill utilization, overqualification effects, person–environment fit, internal mobility, work-sample validity, hidden-worker barriers, and within-firm variation in developmental outcomes have substantial prior literature. |
| Recombined or operationalized differently | PIA's participant-governed evidence chain, capability atoms, and portable outputs; OSI's graph representation of conditions, relationships, capability flow, and state transitions. |
| Open research proposition | Whether evidence that precedes conventional signaling can be inferred and translated into trusted developmental opportunity; whether observable organizational mechanisms can explain capability absorption without collapsing into a score or diagnosis. |

## Source discipline

- A source supports only the bounded claim its design and evidence justify.
- A report about credentials does not establish unrecognized capability.
- A mobility result does not by itself establish the mechanism that produced it.
- A correlation between organizational conditions and outcomes does not establish
  causality without an appropriate design.
- A secondary summary does not replace reading the primary source.
- Temporal validity and temporal jurisdiction must be checked before carrying a
  source into a present-state claim.
