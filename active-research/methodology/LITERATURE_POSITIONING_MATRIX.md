# OSI-PIA Literature-Positioning Matrix

This matrix is a working research instrument for locating OSI and PIA against
existing research traditions. It is not a novelty claim, a systematic review,
or a promotion decision. Rows should be revised as sources are read and
assessed in the [Project Bibliography](../../docs/publications/PROJECT_BIBLIOGRAPHY.md).
The companion [PIA ↔ Open Skills / LER Crosswalk](PIA_LER_OPEN_SKILLS_CROSSWALK.md)
records the separate interoperability question: what belongs in the PIA-native
reasoning layer and what may be translated into established external standards.

## Comparison dimensions

For each tradition, ask:

- What does it measure or explain?
- What is its unit of analysis?
- Is capability observed, self-reported, or inferred?
- Is the environment modeled, and at what scale?
- Is development over time represented?
- Are evidence and outputs portable to the person or organization?
- Is organizational absorption or utilization modeled?
- Where does OSI-PIA overlap?
- What is recombined, operationalized differently, or still an open gap?

## Review protocol

1. Select a tradition and the relevant bibliography IDs.
2. Read the primary source before relying on a secondary summary.
3. Complete the source-assessment fields in the bibliography.
4. Record what the source supports and explicitly what it does not support.
5. Compare the source's time, population, and system conditions with the claim
   being considered.
6. Update this matrix with citations and mark the row's review status.
7. Route any proposed architectural or ontological change through the normal
   evidence, congruence, validation, and governance path.

## Boundary

This matrix helps prevent two symmetrical errors: treating an existing research
tradition as if it already answers the OSI-PIA question, or treating a familiar
recombination as wholly unprecedented. It supports inquiry; it does not confer
authority or promote a research formulation into canonical knowledge.
