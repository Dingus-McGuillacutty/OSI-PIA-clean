# OSI Core Concepts

## Organization

A coordinated human system formed to pursue one or more purposes.

An organization contains people, roles, relationships, structures,
resources, processes, and histories.

---

## Person

A human participant represented within the system.

A Person may hold one or more Roles and may possess multiple
Capabilities.

---

## Role

A set of expected functions, responsibilities, authorities,
and relationships.

A Role may exist independently of the person currently performing it.

---

## Position

A specific organizational placement through which a Role is assigned.

A Position may be:

- filled;
- vacant;
- proposed;
- eliminated;
- reorganized;
- or temporarily covered.

---

## Team

A group of people and roles connected through shared work,
responsibility, or purpose.

---

## Organizational Unit

A formally or informally bounded part of an organization.

Examples:

- department;
- division;
- office;
- program;
- committee;
- project group.

---

## Capability

The ability to perform an action, exercise judgment, solve a problem,
create an output, or produce a useful change.

---

## Capability Capital

The stored human capacity present within the system.

---

## Effective Capability

Capability that is successfully expressed, directed, and applied.

---

## Trust

An emergent relational condition involving expectations of
reliability, safety, competence, honesty, and appropriate behavior.

---

## Predictability

The degree to which participants can form reliable and workable
mental models of the organizational environment.

---

## Organizational Capital

A resource or capacity present within or available to the
organization.

---

## Organizational Energy

The capacity available to initiate, sustain, or redirect action.

---

## Flow

The movement of information, authority, resources, capability,
knowledge, attention, or other organizational elements.

---

## Topology

The structural arrangement of entities and relationships within the
organization.

---

## Field Condition

A distributed condition that influences behavior and movement across
part or all of the organization.

Examples:

- predictability;
- trust;
- psychological safety;
- legitimacy;
- cultural coherence.

---

## Observation

A recorded description of something perceived, detected, or reported.

An Observation should remain distinguishable from interpretation.

---

## Evidence

Information used to support, challenge, or contextualize a claim.

---

## Indicator

An observable measure used as evidence for a broader construct.

---

## Construct

A theoretical property that cannot necessarily be observed directly
but may be inferred from indicators.

Examples:

- organizational health;
- trust;
- predictability;
- resilience.

---

## Assessment

An interpretation of available evidence concerning a person,
relationship, unit, condition, or organizational state.

---

## State

The condition of an entity, relationship, or system at a defined time.

---

## State Transition

A change from one state to another.

---

## Event

An occurrence associated with a time, participants, and possible
state changes.

---

## Outcome

A result produced by organizational activity or state transition.

---

## Organizational Health

An emergent condition reflecting the system’s ability to mobilize
capability, maintain workable relationships, adapt, recover, learn,
and produce sustainable outcomes.

---

## Vacancy

A state in which an established Position lacks its intended occupant.

A Vacancy may create:

- capability loss;
- workload transfer;
- decision delay;
- relationship disruption;
- financial cost;
- and operational risk.
