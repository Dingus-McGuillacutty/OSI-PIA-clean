# OSI-PIA Project Bibliography

This is a living index of external sources cited or linked by the public
OSI-PIA article series. It was assembled from the `Sources` and
`Sources and Related Articles` sections of the articles listed below.

## Reading rule

An entry means that an OSI-PIA article cited or linked the source. It does not
mean that OSI-PIA has independently verified the source, adopted its claims,
or found it current for every purpose. The article remains responsible for
stating what it uses the source to support and what remains uncertain.

This bibliography is a supporting publication artifact. It does not create
canonical terminology, promote research, or replace source-level provenance
and review. A source's temporal jurisdiction remains limited to the period,
population, and conditions it actually describes.

## Evidence-map model

The bibliography is also the intake point for literature positioning. Each
source should be assessed with the following fields before it is used to support
a formal paper, a canonical artifact, or a present-state claim:

- **full citation / DOI / URL** — the precise source locator;
- **source type** — peer-reviewed study, preprint, primary policy or guidance,
  industry/think-tank report, book, conceptual/theoretical work, journalism, or
  practitioner commentary;
- **project scope** — PIA, OSI, or both;
- **relevant constructs** — for example capability, fit, trust, mobility, skill
  utilization, overqualification, learning, internal labor markets, or machine
  participation;
- **study design / sample** — what was actually observed and how;
- **key findings** and **limitations** — reported separately;
- **supports** — the bounded claim the source can help support;
- **does not support** — conclusions the source cannot establish;
- **relationship to the current model** — overlap, recombination, operational
  difference, or possible gap; and
- **review status** — `unread`, `skimmed`, `reviewed`, or `incorporated`.

The current source entries preserve article citations and are intentionally
`cited-unassessed` until those fields are completed. Being listed is not the
same as being read, reviewed, or incorporated.

Use the [Literature-Positioning Matrix](../../active-research/methodology/LITERATURE_POSITIONING_MATRIX.md)
to compare research traditions without treating a familiar label as proof that
the OSI-PIA formulation is either novel or already established.

## Maintenance rules

- Add a source when it is first cited by a public article; do not silently
  replace an existing locator.
- Keep article-to-source relationships visible so a reader can reconstruct why
  a source is present.
- Record a more precise locator when a general homepage was used initially.
- Add publication or access dates when they are available from the source and
  relevant to the claim.
- Recheck temporal validity before reusing a source for a new article or for a
  present-state claim.
- Run the publication encoding, link, and governance checks whenever this
  bibliography changes.
