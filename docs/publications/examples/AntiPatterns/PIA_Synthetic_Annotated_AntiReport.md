# PIA Synthetic Annotated AntiReport

**Purpose:** This document intentionally demonstrates how **not** to write an OSI or PIA participant report. Each example contains a methodological or ethical violation followed by an annotation.

All statements are synthetic methodological examples. They are not a
participant report, a participant dataset, or evidence about an identifiable
person.

This is not a participant-facing report.

---

## Anti-pattern 1: Researcher praise

> Congratulations! Your career history is extremely impressive.

**Why this is a problem**

- Introduces researcher approval into the report.
- Shifts the document from analysis toward affirmation.
- Does not identify evidence or analytical relevance.
- May pressure the participant to accept the researcher's framing.

**Neutral alternative**

> The source material documents recurring expansion of responsibility across technical, educational, mentoring, and quality-related functions.

---

## Anti-pattern 2: Unsupported identity assignment

> You are clearly an exceptional leader.

**Why this is a problem**

- Converts evidence about behavior or assigned responsibility into a fixed identity.
- Uses evaluative language without defined criteria.
- Eliminates uncertainty and alternative interpretations.

**Neutral alternative**

> The record includes repeated instances in which the participant was assigned responsibilities involving guidance, review, mentoring, and coordination.

---

## Anti-pattern 3: Researcher surprise

> We were surprised by how much influence you had without a formal management title.

**Why this is a problem**

- Centers the researcher's expectations.
- Implies that the participant's history violated an unstated norm.
- Adds no analytical value.

**Neutral alternative**

> Several examples describe influence exercised through expertise, mentoring, quality review, and informal coordination rather than through a formal management title.

---

## Anti-pattern 4: Unqualified inference of organizational trust

> Organizations obviously trusted you completely.

**Why this is a problem**

- Treats an interpretation as an established fact.
- Uses absolute language.
- Does not identify the evidence supporting the claim.

**Neutral alternative using the standard structure**

### Evidence

Responsibilities expanded across project delivery, quality review, onboarding,
mentoring, cross-functional support, and guidance of professional practice.

### Observation

These responsibilities involve access to consequential work, influence over practice, and repeated reliance on the participant's judgment.

### Interpretation

The pattern is consistent with organizations assigning increasing professional trust and responsibility over time.

### Confidence

Moderate. The interpretation is supported by multiple independent examples, although direct organizational statements about trust are not present.

---

## Anti-pattern 5: Prescriptive career direction

> You should pursue senior management because that is clearly where you belong.

**Why this is a problem**

- Converts analysis into life or career instruction.
- Assigns an identity and destination to the participant.
- Ignores participant preference, context, and competing interpretations.

**Neutral alternative**

> The material may support reflection on whether the participant wishes to pursue roles with greater formal authority, broader cross-functional scope, deeper specialist responsibility, or some combination of these.

---

## Anti-pattern 6: Claiming proof

> This proves that your employers consistently undervalued you.

**Why this is a problem**

- Claims certainty beyond the evidence.
- Attributes organizational judgment and intent without direct support.
- Collapses potentially different organizations and contexts into one conclusion.

**Neutral alternative**

> The record contains examples in which the participant's documented scope extended beyond the apparent boundaries of the formal role. The available evidence does not establish how each organization valued or interpreted that contribution.

---

## Anti-pattern 7: Giftedness or trait labeling

> You are naturally gifted at seeing systems that other people cannot see.

**Why this is a problem**

- Assigns an internal trait as fact.
- Makes an unsupported comparison with other people.
- Replaces observable patterns with identity language.

**Neutral alternative**

> The source material includes repeated examples of identifying relationships across technical work, process quality, education, and organizational coordination.

---

## Anti-pattern 8: Psychological diagnosis by implication

> You become frustrated because you are too intelligent for ordinary workplaces.

**Why this is a problem**

- Attributes emotion and cause without adequate evidence.
- Creates a hierarchy between the participant and others.
- Uses a broad stereotype rather than source-grounded analysis.

**Neutral alternative**

> The participant described frustration in settings where role boundaries, decision processes, or organizational structures limited cross-functional contribution. Other explanations may also be relevant.

---

## Anti-pattern 9: Flattening uncertainty

> Your pattern is unmistakable.

**Why this is a problem**

- Conceals ambiguity.
- Discourages participant correction or alternative interpretation.
- Presents the analyst's synthesis as final.

**Neutral alternative**

> A recurring pattern appears across several examples. Confidence is moderate because the examples are consistent, although the available source material may not represent every relevant context.

---

## Anti-pattern 10: Reducing a person to a score

> Your leadership score is 92, which means you are a high-potential executive.

**Why this is a problem**

- Converts a complex person into a simplified ranking.
- Presents a model output as identity and destiny.
- Implies predictive validity that may not exist.
- Creates risk of automated judgment or coercive use.

**Neutral alternative**

> The current evidence includes several indicators associated with leadership-related contribution. Any scoring model should remain subordinate to source evidence, stated assumptions, uncertainty, and participant interpretation.

---

## Summary of Violations Demonstrated

This AntiReport intentionally includes:

- praise and criticism;
- unsupported inference;
- identity assignment;
- researcher intrusion;
- prescriptive language;
- hidden uncertainty;
- attribution of intent;
- psychological speculation;
- false proof claims;
- reduction of a participant to a score.

The correct OSI/PIA approach is to preserve evidence, provenance, uncertainty, dignity, and participant authority while clearly separating observation from interpretation.
