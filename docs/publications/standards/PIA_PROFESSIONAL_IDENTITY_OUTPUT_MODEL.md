# PIA Professional Identity and Resume Output Model

**Status:** Working standard
**Version:** 0.2.0
**Domain:** Professional Identity Architecture (PIA)

## Status and purpose

This document records the standard working model for PIA resume-style outputs.
Version 0.2 incorporates the structure of the fourth-generation private DOCX
reference while preserving the distinction between a capability-centered
professional identity artifact, a conventional chronological resume, and a
participant development review.

The current private reference is a participant-specific DOCX. The reference
file, its identifying content, and its distinctive personal narrative are not
stored in this public clean repository. This standard records only reusable
structure and presentation rules. The example below is synthetic and redacted.

## Working rule

PIA resume outputs translate reviewed evidence into a participant-facing
professional identity document organized around demonstrated capability,
purpose, role meaning, impact, and learning—not only accomplishments or
chronological job history.

The resume is one governed traversal through the participant's professional
identity representation. It is not the identity model itself.

## Three PIA output lanes

### 1. Professional identity or resume output

A participant-facing, capability-centered career artifact suitable for
professional use. Its organization emphasizes purpose, contribution,
demonstrated capability, impact, and development across experience.

### 2. Participant development or review output

A reflective document intended to help the participant understand evidence,
capabilities, patterns, limits, and possible development opportunities. It is
not automatically a resume and may contain review context inappropriate for a
public career document.

### 3. Methodology and governance output

Documentation describing how PIA operates, including evidence standards,
consent, participant authority, assurance, governance, and research methods.
These documents explain the system and do not represent an individual.

## Standard output architecture

The professional identity document should normally use this sequence:

1. **Identity header** — participant-approved name or public label, concise
   professional descriptor, and only the contact or public-link fields approved
   for the intended release.
2. **Professional thesis** — a short first-person statement connecting purpose,
   capability, contribution, and the conditions in which the participant
   creates value.
3. **Core capability areas** — a selective, readable set of capability areas
   supported by reviewed evidence. This is not a keyword inventory or a score
   display.
4. **Professional experience** — one or more roles or bodies of work, each
   presented with enough organizational and chronological context to orient the
   reader, followed by:
   - **Purpose** — why the work mattered or what condition it was intended to
     improve;
   - **What I Did** — concise, evidence-grounded actions and responsibilities;
   - **Impact** — bounded outcomes, improvements, or effects supported by the
     evidence; and
   - **What I Learned** — reflective development, transferable understanding,
     or refined professional judgment.
5. **Leadership and governance** — selected service, stewardship, advisory,
   governance, or leadership activity relevant to the represented professional
   identity.
6. **Certifications and licenses** — verified or appropriately qualified
   credentials, without treating possession as automatic proof of applied
   competence.
7. **Professional development** — relevant training, study, and continuing
   development represented separately from demonstrated capability.
8. **Education** — concise educational context appropriate to the intended
   audience.

Chronology may still appear where useful, but it is supporting context rather
than the organizing theory of professional identity.

## Experience-entry grammar

Each experience entry should tell a coherent professional-development story:

> **Role or body of work**
>
> **Purpose:** What condition, need, or mission gave the work meaning?
>
> **What I Did:** What participant-supported actions or responsibilities can be
> stated plainly?
>
> **Impact:** What changed, improved, continued, or became possible within the
> documented scope?
>
> **What I Learned:** What transferable understanding or professional judgment
> developed through the experience?

This grammar is a participant-facing translation of reviewed evidence. It must
not invent causality, ownership, scope, or results that the evidence does not
support.

## Presentation model

The fourth-generation reference establishes a restrained resume presentation:

- a clean, single-column reading order;
- a prominent identity header followed immediately by the professional thesis;
- short, visually distinct section headings;
- compact capability and accomplishment lists;
- repeated experience-entry labels that make purpose, action, impact, and
  learning easy to scan;
- conventional chronology retained inside experience entries rather than used
  as the document's primary theory;
- no capability scores, personality rankings, graph internals, reviewer-only
  fields, or assurance-system jargon in the participant-facing artifact; and
- no decorative complexity that competes with evidence, meaning, or readable
  professional identity.

The layout must preserve a logical reading order in DOCX, PDF, HTML, and
assistive technology. Formatting may change by medium, but the section order,
meaning, and evidence boundaries must remain stable.

> **Synthetic Participant — Professional Identity (Working Draft)**
>
> **Professional thesis**
>
> I improve complex operational systems by preserving knowledge, clarifying
> decisions, and helping people build the capability needed to sustain change.
>
> **Core capability areas**
>
> - Operational systems improvement
> - Knowledge preservation and transfer
> - Cross-functional coordination
> - Professional learning and capability development
>
> **Selected experience area — Operational coordination**
>
> **Purpose:** Improve continuity and reduce avoidable coordination loss in a
> changing operational environment.
>
> **What I Did:** Developed repeatable handoff practices, maintained critical
> operating knowledge, and coordinated work across multiple roles.
>
> **Impact:** Improved continuity and made operating knowledge easier for other
> team members to find and use within the documented work setting.
>
> **What I Learned:** Sustainable improvement depends on both sound process and
> the confidence of people who must use, adapt, and maintain it.
>
> **Evidence boundary:** This evidence supports a bounded representation of
> coordination and knowledge-transfer capability. It does not independently
> establish authority, universal performance, or results outside the documented
> context.

This example contains no real participant name, employer, location, dates,
contact information, credential numbers, filenames, or source identifiers.

## Evidence and assurance requirements

- Every represented capability must remain traceable to participant-authorized,
  reviewed evidence.
- Evidence review and capability interpretation remain distinct decisions.
- Repetition across documents does not automatically increase evidentiary
  weight.
- Credential completion does not automatically establish applied capability.
- Participant-readable language must not erase uncertainty or negative
  boundaries.
- Impact statements must distinguish documented effects from inferred or
  participant-reported effects.
- The output must not expose reviewer notes, internal confidence mechanics,
  protected source identifiers, or graph implementation details.
- The participant must be able to review and challenge the representation
  before release.
- Technical provenance may accompany the participant document but should not
  dominate the professional identity narrative.

## Format and privacy boundary

Markdown is the canonical repository source for this model and its reusable
rules. DOCX is the current reference distribution format for the professional
identity artifact. RTF remains a legacy working format. PDF and HTML may be
generated from the governed source after format, privacy, and accessibility
validation.

The private participant DOCX is an input to standard development, not a public
repository artifact. Public examples must be purpose-built with synthetic data
or separately consented, publication-approved participant material.

## Current maturity boundary

This document records a working standard and does not claim empirical
validation of career outcomes, hiring effectiveness, predictive accuracy, or
generalizability. The fourth-generation reference demonstrates a coherent
output structure; it does not by itself validate the structure across
participants, professions, or audiences. The next stage is repeatable generation
from fully reviewed synthetic and participant-authorized evidence, followed by
privacy, accessibility, participant-usability, and audience-fit review.
