# PIA Professional Identity and Resume Output Model

**Status:** Working draft
**Version:** 0.1.0
**Domain:** Professional Identity Architecture (PIA)

## Status and purpose

This document records the current working model for PIA resume-style outputs.
It defines a capability-centered professional identity artifact rather than a
conventional chronological resume or a participant development review.

The private working reference was an RTF professional-identity draft created
during early PIA development. Its participant-identifying content is not
reproduced in this public clean repository. The example below is synthetic and
redacted.

## Working rule

PIA resume outputs translate reviewed evidence into a participant-facing
professional identity document organized around demonstrated capability,
purpose, role meaning, and learning—not only accomplishments or chronological
job history.

The resume is one governed traversal through the participant's professional
identity representation. It is not the identity model itself.

## Three PIA output lanes

### 1. Professional identity or resume output

A participant-facing, capability-centered career artifact suitable for
professional use. Its organization emphasizes purpose, contribution,
demonstrated capability, and development across experience.

### 2. Participant development or review output

A reflective document intended to help the participant understand evidence,
capabilities, patterns, limits, and possible development opportunities. It is
not automatically a resume and may contain review context inappropriate for a
public career document.

### 3. Methodology and governance output

Documentation describing how PIA operates, including evidence standards,
consent, participant authority, assurance, governance, and research methods.
These documents explain the system and do not represent an individual.

## Professional identity output structure

The working professional identity document should normally include:

1. **Professional identity heading** — participant-approved name or public
   label and a plain-language identity statement.
2. **Working thesis** — a concise statement connecting purpose, capability,
   contribution, and the conditions in which the participant creates value.
3. **Capability overview** — a bounded set of capabilities supported by
   reviewed evidence.
4. **Experience narratives** — major roles or bodies of work expressed through:
   - Purpose;
   - What I Did; and
   - What I Learned.
5. **Selected evidence or outcomes** — source-grounded examples appropriate to
   the intended audience.
6. **Learning and development** — credentials, training, and experiential
   learning represented without treating completion as automatic competence.
7. **Evidence boundaries** — concise limits needed to prevent inflation or
   unsupported inference.

Chronology may still appear where useful, but it is supporting context rather
than the organizing theory of professional identity.

## Synthetic, redacted example model

> **Synthetic Participant — Professional Identity (Working Draft)**
>
> **Working thesis**
>
> I improve complex operational systems by preserving knowledge, clarifying
> decisions, and helping people build the capability needed to sustain change.
>
> **Capability overview**
>
> - Operational systems improvement
> - Knowledge preservation and transfer
> - Cross-functional coordination
> - Professional learning and capability development
>
> **Selected experience area — Operational coordination**
>
> **Purpose:** Improve continuity and reduce avoidable coordination loss in a
> changing operational environment.
>
> **What I Did:** Developed repeatable handoff practices, maintained critical
> operating knowledge, and coordinated work across multiple roles.
>
> **What I Learned:** Sustainable improvement depends on both sound process and
> the confidence of people who must use, adapt, and maintain it.
>
> **Evidence boundary:** This evidence supports a bounded representation of
> coordination and knowledge-transfer capability. It does not independently
> establish authority, universal performance, or results outside the documented
> context.

This example contains no real participant name, employer, location, dates,
contact information, credential numbers, filenames, or source identifiers.

## Evidence and assurance requirements

- Every represented capability must remain traceable to participant-authorized,
  reviewed evidence.
- Evidence review and capability interpretation remain distinct decisions.
- Repetition across documents does not automatically increase evidentiary
  weight.
- Credential completion does not automatically establish applied capability.
- Participant-readable language must not erase uncertainty or negative
  boundaries.
- The participant must be able to review and challenge the representation
  before release.
- Technical provenance may accompany the participant document but should not
  dominate the professional identity narrative.

## Format

Markdown is the canonical repository source for the model and reusable
template. RTF is an approved working distribution format for the professional
identity artifact. DOCX, PDF, and HTML may be generated later after format and
accessibility validation.

## Current maturity boundary

This document records a working output model and does not claim empirical
validation of career outcomes, hiring effectiveness, predictive accuracy, or
generalizability. The next stage is generation from fully reviewed synthetic
and participant-authorized evidence, followed by participant usability review.
