# OSI Publication Standard v0.1

**Status:** Working standard  
**Project:** Organizational Systems Intelligence (OSI) / Professional Identity Architecture (PIA)

## Purpose

This standard establishes a consistent method for producing OSI and PIA publications that are evidence-grounded, traceable, ethically framed, and suitable for review.

The standard applies to participant reviews, technical reports, organizational reports, validation reports, methodology papers, governance documents, and working papers.

## Core Principles

### 1. Evidence before interpretation

Publications must distinguish source material from analytical judgment. Claims should be traceable to identifiable evidence.

### 2. Interpretation before conclusion

Interpretation should explain what the evidence may indicate without presenting inference as fact.

### 3. Conclusion before action

Recommendations or possible actions should follow from documented evidence and interpretation rather than appearing as unsupported prescriptions.

### 4. Narrative Neutrality

OSI publications describe rather than praise, criticize, diagnose, or assign identity. Neutrality is not emotional distance from participants; it is disciplined separation of evidence, interpretation, and researcher reaction.

### 5. Provenance preservation

Source facts, participant statements, derived observations, and later interpretations should remain distinguishable.

### 6. Participant authority

Participants remain authoritative regarding their own lived experience. OSI analysis may identify patterns, but it does not replace participant interpretation of self.

### 7. Epistemic integrity

Epistemic integrity shall be preserved throughout every stage of analysis and publication.

Publications must distinguish what is directly known, what is inferred, how strongly each assertion is supported, and what remains uncertain. No transformation, summary, visualization, or editorial decision may silently strengthen, weaken, reinterpret, or fabricate knowledge.

Every material conclusion must remain traceable to its supporting evidence and reproducible through the documented analytical process.

## Narrative Neutrality Requirements

Publications should:

- describe rather than evaluate;
- separate evidence from interpretation;
- avoid researcher emotion and surprise;
- avoid praise or criticism;
- avoid assigning identities or fixed traits;
- avoid prescriptive career or life direction;
- preserve uncertainty;
- preserve participant dignity;
- preserve the distinction between facts, observations, inferences, conclusions, and unknowns;
- avoid increasing confidence beyond what the evidence supports.

## Standard Analytical Structure

When interpretation is present, use the following sequence where practical:

### Evidence

What source material, observed event, documented responsibility, or participant statement supports the analysis?

### Observation

What recurring feature, relationship, sequence, or change is visible in the evidence?

### Interpretation

What may the observation indicate within the OSI or PIA framework?

### Confidence

How strongly is the interpretation supported?

Suggested confidence labels:

- **Low** — limited, ambiguous, or single-source support.
- **Moderate** — multiple examples or partially independent support.
- **High** — repeated, convergent, and well-documented support.

Confidence describes evidentiary support, not the worth or credibility of a participant.

Unknown or insufficiently supported matters must remain explicitly unknown or unresolved rather than being completed through assumption.

## Publication Families

| Code | Publication family | Primary purpose |
|---|---|---|
| PR | Participant Review | Return a structured, neutral synthesis to a participant |
| TR | Technical Report | Document technical analysis, models, architecture, or implementation |
| OR | Organizational Report | Describe organizational patterns, conditions, risks, and system behavior |
| VR | Validation Report | Test reliability, validity, reproducibility, or model performance |
| MP | Methodology Paper | Define analytical methods, constructs, assumptions, and limitations |
| GD | Governance Document | Establish ethics, policy, standards, decision rights, or safeguards |
| WP | Working Paper | Preserve provisional research, hypotheses, and developing concepts |

## Recommended Document Structure

1. Title and document identifier
2. Version and status
3. Purpose
4. Scope
5. Source material or evidence base
6. Method
7. Findings or recurring patterns
8. Evidence–Observation–Interpretation–Confidence sections
9. Limitations and uncertainty
10. Implications or opportunities for reflection
11. Revision history

Not every publication requires every section. Omissions should reflect the document's purpose rather than convenience.

## Publication Ethics

OSI and PIA publications must:

- never infer intent without evidence;
- distinguish observed behavior from inferred capability;
- distinguish organizational assignment from personal traits;
- communicate uncertainty;
- make interpretations traceable;
- preserve source provenance through publication;
- label inference as inference;
- keep unknowns and unresolved questions visible;
- avoid artificial inflation of confidence;
- avoid identity language presented as analytical fact;
- avoid surveillance, coercive control, automated judgment, or reduction of people to simplistic scores;
- preserve dignity, context, and the possibility of alternative explanations.

Ethical responsibility includes epistemic responsibility.

## Relationship Between OSI and PIA

PIA publications primarily preserve and interpret person-level evidence, experience, capability, development, and participation.

OSI publications primarily examine organizational conditions, structures, relationships, flows, state transitions, and outcomes.

PIA evidence may inform OSI analysis, but person-level evidence must not be converted into organizational conclusions without an explicit interpretive bridge and stated uncertainty.

## Canonical and Generated Formats

Markdown is the canonical repository format for publication standards and source documents.

RTF, DOCX, PDF, HTML, and other presentation formats should be treated as generated or distribution artifacts unless explicitly designated otherwise.

## Versioning

- Increment the patch version for corrections that do not change meaning.
- Increment the minor version for added sections, clarified requirements, or expanded scope.
- Increment the major version for substantial methodological or governance changes.
