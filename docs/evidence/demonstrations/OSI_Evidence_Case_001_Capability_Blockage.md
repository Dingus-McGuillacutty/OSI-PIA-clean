# Evidence Case 001 — Capability Blockage

## Research Question

Can an organization possess sufficient capability while simultaneously failing
to utilize it effectively?

## Purpose

Demonstrate how OSI distinguishes between capability deficit, capability
blockage, and capability suppression without reducing the analysis to
individual performance.

## Synthetic Scenario

An operational team exhibits declining output despite stable staffing,
unchanged workload, consistent technical qualifications, and no significant
organizational restructuring.

Traditional interpretations might suggest declining performance,
disengagement, or inadequate capability.

## OSI Observations

### Structural Conditions

- Decision pathways are becoming increasingly centralized.
- Dependency is concentrating around key roles.
- Coordination friction is increasing.

### Predictability Conditions

- Role clarity is reduced.
- Operational expectations are inconsistent.
- Decision cycles are longer.

### Trust Conditions

- Voluntary information sharing is declining.
- Initiative is reduced.
- Escalation behavior is increasing.

### Capability Conditions

Capability remains present. The primary constraint is system utilization rather
than capability absence.

## Interpretation

The observed performance degradation is more consistent with **blocked
capability** than missing capability. The organization possesses necessary
capability, but current system conditions prevent effective utilization.

## Why This Matters

Treating blockage as a capability deficit may result in unnecessary hiring,
inappropriate performance management, and continued degradation of
organizational trust. OSI instead recommends investigating system conditions
before concluding that capability deficiencies exist.

## Evidence Boundary

Synthetic demonstration for research and validation purposes only. It does not
evaluate individuals, assign responsibility, establish causality, or authorize
a consequential organizational decision.
