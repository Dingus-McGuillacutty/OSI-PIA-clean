# Evidence Case 003 — Misattribution of Failure

## Research Question

How might organizational failures be incorrectly attributed to individuals when
system conditions are the primary cause?

## Purpose

Demonstrate OSI's ability to distinguish individual performance concerns from
organizational condition failures.

## Synthetic Scenario

A team experiences missed deadlines, inconsistent quality, and increasing
operational friction. Management responds by focusing on individual
accountability.

## OSI Observations

### Structural Conditions

- Responsibility boundaries are unclear.
- Authority is inconsistent with responsibility.
- Multiple conflicting reporting paths exist.

### Predictability Conditions

- Expectations change.
- Communication is inconsistent.
- Operations are ambiguous.

### Trust Conditions

- Confidence is declining.
- Defensive behavior is increasing.
- Initiative is reduced.

### Flow Conditions

- Rework is increasing.
- Communication is being lost.
- Coordination failures are repeated.

## Interpretation

There is no evidence in this synthetic case that individual performance alone
explains the outcomes. The observed failures are more consistent with
organizational condition failures than individual capability deficiencies.

## Why This Matters

Misattribution may produce unnecessary disciplinary action, erosion of trust,
loss of capable personnel, and persistence of underlying organizational
problems. OSI recommends correcting system conditions before assigning
individual responsibility.

## Evidence Boundary

Synthetic demonstration supporting diagnostic research. It does not evaluate
or rank an individual, establish causality, or authorize a personnel decision.
