# Evidence Case 002 — False Capability Signal

## Research Question

Can strong organizational performance conceal deteriorating system health?

## Purpose

Demonstrate how sustained output may represent organizational fragility rather
than organizational strength.

## Evidence Classification

| Field | Classification |
|---|---|
| Classification | Synthetic Demonstration |
| Purpose | Method and diagnostic-framework validation |
| Research themes | Capability; Trust; Resilience |
| Evidence level | Synthetic |
| Claims supported | The framework can distinguish observable output from underlying organizational resilience in a bounded scenario |
| Claims not supported | Real-world prevalence; predictive accuracy; causal effectiveness; generalizability; evaluation of any real individual or organization |
| Next validation stage | Controlled synthetic variation, adversarial or confounded test cases, then empirical validation with governed real-world data |

## Synthetic Scenario

A department consistently delivers projects on schedule. Leadership concludes
that performance is strong, coordination is effective, and organizational
health is stable.

## OSI Observations

### Structural Conditions

- Dependence is increasing around a small number of individuals.
- Informal decision routing is replacing documented processes.
- Expertise is becoming hidden and centralized.

### Predictability Conditions

- Tacit knowledge dominates operations.
- Documentation is limited.
- Succession resilience is poor.

### Trust Conditions

- Local trust is strong.
- Organization-wide visibility is weak.
- Responsibility is increasingly concentrated.

### Capability Conditions

Performance remains high while system resilience declines.

## Interpretation

OSI identifies an **illusion of capability strength**: current performance is
maintained through concentrated expertise rather than resilient organizational
conditions.

## Why This Matters

Without recognizing these conditions, organizations risk sudden operational
failure, knowledge loss, succession instability, and hidden technical debt. OSI
separates observable performance from organizational resilience.

## Evidence Boundary

Synthetic demonstration only. It does not evaluate individuals, establish
organizational health, prove causality, or support a consequential decision.
