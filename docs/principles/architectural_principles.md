# OSI Architectural Principles

Version: 1.0  
Status: Stable

## Purpose

These principles guide the development of Organizational Systems Intelligence. They are intended to outlive individual implementations and change only when the underlying philosophy of the platform changes.

## 1. Assurance before ingestion

No information enters the platform until it has passed an explicit assurance process.

## 2. Evidence before inference

Every analytical conclusion should be supported by traceable evidence. OSI prefers incomplete evidence over unsupported certainty.

## 3. Explainability before automation

Every significant conclusion should be understandable by a human reviewer. Automation assists judgment; it does not replace judgment.

## 4. Traceability before intelligence

Every Finding should show what evidence was evaluated, what rule was applied, and why the conclusion was reached.

## 5. Ethics as a first-class concern

Participant consent is evaluated independently of technical correctness. Ethical authorization precedes organizational authorization.

## 6. Stable contracts, flexible implementations

Component contracts evolve cautiously. Implementations may evolve continuously within those contracts.

## 7. Organizations are living systems

OSI models organizations as dynamic cooperative systems shaped by relationships, capability, trust, movement, learning, and adaptation.

## 8. Intelligence emerges from relationships

Meaning exists within relationships rather than isolated records. Graph structures represent organizational behavior more faithfully than hierarchy alone.

## 9. Preserve human agency

Every recommendation should remain reviewable, challengeable, and explainable.

## 10. Architecture is a public contract

Implementation details may change. Stable architectural commitments should remain dependable.
