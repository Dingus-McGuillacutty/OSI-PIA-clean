# ADR-OSI-0001: Model Organizations as Living Cooperative Systems

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-07-17

## Context

Traditional organizational models often represent organizations as
machines, charts, processes, or collections of positions.

These models are useful but incomplete.

They do not adequately represent:

- adaptation;
- trust;
- motivation;
- emergence;
- relational behavior;
- informal structure;
- human judgment;
- and changing environmental conditions.

## Decision

OSI will model organizations as living cooperative human systems.

Machine, network, ecological, and biological metaphors may be used as
analytical tools, but none will be treated as a complete representation.

## Rationale

Organizational outcomes emerge through interactions among people,
relationships, structures, resources, histories, and environmental
conditions.

A living-systems orientation better supports the study of:

- health;
- adaptation;
- state transitions;
- capability;
- trust;
- flow;
- and resilience.

## Consequences

OSI must:

- examine relationships as well as formal structure;
- represent changing states;
- distinguish stored capability from expressed capability;
- account for environmental and relational conditions;
- avoid reducing people to mechanical components;
- and treat organizational health as emergent.

## Alternatives considered

### Organization as machine

Useful for workflows and process analysis but insufficient for
emergent human behavior.

### Organization as static hierarchy

Useful for formal authority but insufficient for informal flow,
trust, adaptation, and cross-functional capability.

### Organization as network only

Useful for relationships but insufficient without state, field,
energy, and health concepts.
