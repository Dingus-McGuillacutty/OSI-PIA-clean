# PIA Meta-Model

## Purpose

The Professional Identity Architecture (PIA) Meta-Model defines the conceptual
architecture for representing professional identity as a governed,
evidence-based system.

PIA provides a structured framework for organizing, interpreting, and governing
participant-authorized professional evidence while preserving human agency,
transparency, explainability, and participant control.

The PIA Meta-Model is intentionally independent of implementation technology
and serves as the canonical conceptual foundation for all PIA components.

---

# Relationship to the OSI Foundation

Professional Identity Architecture is a specialized domain within the broader
Organizational Systems Intelligence (OSI) architecture.

The three foundational models operate together:

- **OSI Development Model** defines how trustworthy knowledge develops.
- **OSI Meta-Model** defines how cooperative systems are represented.
- **PIA Meta-Model** defines how professional identity is represented within
  those systems.

PIA inherits the epistemology, governance, and assurance principles established
by the OSI foundation.

---

# Foundational Premise

Professional identity is not a résumé.

Professional identity is not a collection of credentials.

Professional identity is not a score.

Professional identity is an evidence-governed representation that emerges from
relationships among participant-authorized evidence, experience, capability,
context, governance, and interpretation.

Identity is therefore understood as an evolving representation rather than a
static artifact.

---

# Foundational Principles

## Participant Agency

The participant is the authoritative owner of their professional evidence.

Participation is voluntary.

Evidence submission, review, amendment, and withdrawal remain participant
controlled.

---

## Evidence Before Interpretation

Evidence exists independently of interpretation.

PIA preserves this distinction throughout the architecture.

Interpretations should remain explainable, reviewable, and traceable to their
supporting evidence.

---

## Explainability

Professional representations should remain understandable by both participants
and reviewers.

The system should be able to explain how conclusions were reached and which
evidence contributed to those conclusions.

---

## Governance

Professional identity is governed rather than inferred.

Governance defines how evidence is reviewed, interpreted, promoted, corrected,
and maintained over time.

---

## Human Review

PIA supports human judgment rather than replacing it.

Automation may assist organization, discovery, and analysis, but professional
identity remains subject to participant review and governed oversight.

---

# Core Concepts

PIA models relationships among several foundational concepts.

## Participant

The individual whose professional identity is represented.

Participants remain distinct from their records, profiles, or analytical
representations.

---

## Evidence

Participant-authorized source material.

Examples include:

- education;
- employment;
- certifications;
- military service;
- publications;
- projects;
- professional writing;
- training;
- awards;
- portfolios.

Evidence represents observable artifacts rather than conclusions.

---

## Credentials

Verified formal achievements recognized by authoritative organizations.

Credentials represent one category of evidence rather than the entirety of
professional identity.

---

## Experience

Documented participation in professional activities.

Experience provides context for interpreting evidence but does not independently
establish capability.

---

## Capability

Capability represents a governed interpretation of evidence.

Capability is not assumed.

Capability is not predicted.

Capability remains proportional to available evidence.

---

## Context

Professional evidence exists within organizational, historical, cultural, and
situational contexts.

Context influences interpretation while remaining distinguishable from the
evidence itself.

---

## Professional Identity

Professional identity is the emergent representation produced through governed
relationships among evidence, credentials, experience, capability, context,
participant review, and organizational understanding.

---

# Identity Lifecycle

Professional identity develops through a governed process.

Evidence

↓

Verification

↓

Interpretation

↓

Structured Knowledge

↓

Capability Representation

↓

Participant Review

↓

Governed Professional Identity

Identity therefore remains dynamic rather than static.

---

# Knowledge Architecture

PIA distinguishes several forms of knowledge.

Evidence remains the primary source.

Interpretation produces structured understanding.

Governance evaluates interpretations.

Participant review maintains agency.

Canonical representations emerge only after appropriate governance and review.

---

# Scope

The PIA Meta-Model does not prescribe:

- user interface design;
- graph database implementation;
- artificial intelligence techniques;
- storage technologies;
- visualization methods.

These are implementation choices.

The Meta-Model defines the conceptual architecture they implement.

---

# Relationship to OSI

OSI models organizations as cooperative systems.

PIA models professional identity within those systems.

Together they provide complementary perspectives:

OSI asks:

> How do organizations function?

PIA asks:

> How do professionals develop, represent, and contribute capability within
> those organizations?

Neither replaces the other.

Each enriches the understanding of the other through shared evidence,
governance, and knowledge development.

---

# Closing Statement

The PIA Meta-Model provides a stable conceptual foundation for representing
professional identity while allowing technologies, analytical methods,
interfaces, and implementation strategies to evolve.

Its purpose is not to automate professional judgment.

Its purpose is to create an explainable, participant-centered, and
evidence-governed architecture through which professional identity can be
represented responsibly.
