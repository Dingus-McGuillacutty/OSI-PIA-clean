# Canonical Terminology

## Assurance

The structured process used to determine whether information is suitable for ingestion or downstream use within OSI.

## Assurance Dimension

A category of evaluation performed by an Assurance Engine.

## Assurance Report

The canonical public output of an Assurance Engine.

## Assurance Result

The summarized outcome of one assurance dimension.

## Architectural Affordance

A structural property of the knowledge architecture that enables or constrains
permissible human and machine actions without requiring procedural instruction.

## Canonical Knowledge

Knowledge that has completed the required promotion process and is recognized as the current authoritative project reference until revised, superseded, deprecated, or retired.

## Capability Capital

The accumulated organizational capability represented by individuals, relationships, experience, demonstrated evidence, and the conditions that enable capability to be used.

## Component Contract

The standardized interface implemented by an OSI assurance component.

## Congruence

The consistency of related facts, references, and relationships across datasets or system representations.

Within the Knowledge Management Architecture, congruence means compatibility without contradiction across the domains relevant to a formulation.

## Epistemic Integrity

The preservation of evidence, provenance, uncertainty, traceability, and explainability.

## Finding

A structured observation produced during assurance execution.

## Graph Assurance

The assurance process applied to organizational graph structures and relationships.

## Knowledge Lifecycle

The seven-state maturation model governing how knowledge progresses through Observation, Exploration, Formulation, Congruence, Validation, Promotion, and Stewardship.

The lifecycle governs knowledge regardless of the artifact carrying it.

## Knowledge Management

The discipline of creating, organizing, evaluating, promoting, stewarding, evolving, and retiring knowledge throughout the OSI/PIA ecosystem.

## Knowledge Management Architecture

The cross-cutting architecture consisting of the Knowledge Lifecycle, Congruence Protocol, Knowledge Management Governance, Assurance, and Stewardship.

It defines how knowledge matures, how readiness is evaluated, how transitions are governed, and how promoted knowledge is maintained.

## Knowledge Management Governance

The policies, responsibilities, evidence requirements, authority, and decision processes governing movement of knowledge through the Knowledge Lifecycle and the maintenance of canonical knowledge.

## Logic Chain

The recorded reasoning sequence connecting evidence, rule evaluation, and a Finding.

## Organizational Health

The condition of an organization inferred from evidence about relationships, capability, trust, movement, structure, outcomes, and state change.

## Organizational Metabolism

The movement of people, capability, knowledge, and work through an organization over time.

## Participant Package

The canonical collection of CSV files describing a participant, sources, experiences, evidence, capabilities, and evidence-to-capability mappings.

## Promotion

The advancement of knowledge to a higher-confidence state based on sufficient evidence, congruence review, validation, and appropriate authority.

Promotion represents increased justified confidence rather than certainty.

## State Transition

A measurable or evidence-supported change in the condition of an organizational system over time.

Within the Knowledge Lifecycle, a state transition is a justified movement of knowledge from one lifecycle state to another.

## Stewardship

The ongoing responsibility to maintain, review, improve, version, correct, deprecate, supersede, or retire promoted knowledge while preserving provenance and traceability.

## Trust

An ecological system property influencing cooperation, motivation, capability utilization, resilience, and organizational performance.

## Knowledge Management Relationships

- **Knowledge Lifecycle** defines knowledge states.
- **Congruence Protocol** evaluates compatibility, contradiction, and readiness for further validation.
- **Knowledge Management Governance** governs transition policy, evidence requirements, authority, and decision records.
- **Assurance** evaluates information, structures, and implementations and may provide evidence for lifecycle transitions.
- **Stewardship** maintains promoted knowledge over time.
