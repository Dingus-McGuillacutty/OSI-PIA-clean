# OSI Knowledge Architecture

## Purpose

The OSI Knowledge Architecture defines how knowledge is created, represented,
governed, and applied throughout the Organizational Systems Intelligence
platform.

It provides the conceptual structure that connects research, evidence,
governance, architecture, implementation, and participant understanding into a
single coherent knowledge system.

The Knowledge Architecture is independent of implementation technologies and
serves as the highest-level conceptual map of the OSI foundation.

---

# Foundational Principle

OSI treats knowledge as a governed system rather than a static collection of
documents.

Knowledge develops through observable state changes.

Representations evolve through evidence.

Governance preserves integrity.

Architecture preserves coherence.

Implementation operationalizes knowledge.

---

# Four Complementary Foundations

The OSI foundation consists of four complementary architectural models.

## 1. OSI Development Model

Defines how trustworthy knowledge develops.

Primary question:

> How does knowledge become trustworthy?

---

## 2. OSI Meta-Model

Defines the conceptual architecture of human cooperative systems.

Primary question:

> What is the system being represented?

---

## 3. PIA Meta-Model

Defines the conceptual architecture of professional identity.

Primary question:

> How is professional identity represented within cooperative systems?

---

## 4. Knowledge Lifecycle

Defines the states through which knowledge progresses over time.

Primary question:

> What state is this knowledge currently in?

---

# Architectural Relationships

Each foundational model represents a different architectural dimension.

| Foundation | Dimension |
|------------|-----------|
| Development Model | Process |
| Meta-Model | Structure |
| Knowledge Lifecycle | State |
| Governance | Authority |
| Assurance | Verification |
| Research | Exploration |
| Evidence | Observation |

These dimensions are complementary rather than hierarchical.

Together they form the Knowledge Architecture.

---

# Knowledge Development

Knowledge evolves through governed state transitions.

Observation

↓

Research Question

↓

Experimentation

↓

Evidence

↓

Congruence

↓

Governance

↓

Canonical Knowledge

↓

Implementation

↓

New Observation

The architecture is therefore recursive rather than linear.

---

# Ontological Pluralism

The Knowledge Architecture supports multiple interacting ontologies.

Rather than collapsing disciplinary perspectives into a single vocabulary,
knowledge domains preserve their individual integrity while establishing
governed mappings through evidence and congruence.

This approach supports explainability, traceability, and responsible evolution
of knowledge.

---

# Separation of Concerns

OSI intentionally distinguishes:

- evidence from interpretation;
- research from architecture;
- governance from implementation;
- participant agency from automation;
- representation from prediction.

These distinctions preserve clarity while allowing increasingly sophisticated
analysis.

---

# Platform Independence

The Knowledge Architecture does not prescribe:

- databases;
- graph technologies;
- programming languages;
- artificial intelligence;
- user interfaces;
- storage mechanisms.

These implement the architecture.

They do not define it.

---

# Vision

OSI is intended to function as a governed knowledge environment.

Its objective is not merely to analyze organizations or represent professional
identity.

Its objective is to provide an explainable architecture through which
trustworthy knowledge may be developed, represented, governed, and applied
across multiple domains while preserving human agency and evidence integrity.
